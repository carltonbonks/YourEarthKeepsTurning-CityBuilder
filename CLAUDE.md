# CLAUDE.md — City-Builder Roguelite (working title TBD)

This file tells Claude what game we're building, what matters, and how to work with Yusuf.
It's the source of truth for design intent. When code or docs drift from it, flag it and don't quietly diverge.

## Elevator pitch

You're a hands-off ruler of a frontier town. You never command units. You shape the town with buildings, production chains, incentives and drafted relics, and your heroes decide for themselves how to fight, what to loot and where to venture. Runs are 30–90 minute roguelites played in rounds, auto-battler style (like How Many Dudes). You make a few weighty decisions between rounds, then watch the systems play out.

**Reference games:** Majesty / Lessaria (indirect hero control), Against the Storm (runs, production chains, meta unlocks), Ratropolis / How Many Dudes (round-based auto-battles), Slay the Spire (relic drafting), They Are Billions / Frostpunk (pressure, defences, harsh world), Dwarf Fortress / Rimworld (emergent stories), Cult of the Lamb / Moonlighter (hub town), Loop Hero, Hades, Path of Exile 2 (itemisation).

## Design pillars (in priority order)

1. **Incremental power choices.** The roguelite drip of picking upgrades and relics that stack into a build. Every between-round decision should feel like it moves the needle.
2. **Emergent systems you watch unfold.** Heroes, townsfolk, enemies and the environment interact in ways the player didn't script. Favour systemic rules over hand-authored outcomes.
3. **Power now vs. power later.** Choices should regularly trade immediate safety against long-term scaling (economy, hero growth, relic synergies).
4. **Loot and itemisation.** Random drops, item traits and gear progression, with heroes choosing their own gear wherever that's reasonable. That keeps the player hands-off.
5. **Balancing NPC whims.** The player's job is to keep self-interested heroes and townsfolk pointed in a useful direction.

When a feature doesn't serve at least one pillar, question it.

**Tenet: make it land (Yusuf, 2026-10-26).** Successes and losses the player experiences should feel impactful in every way: mechanically (they change what the run can do) and in how they are shown (the screen and the interface give them a beat of their own: a pause, a highlight, a sound or a line, never a menu snapping open over them). Example: when a night's last raider falls, time slows for a moment on the kill before the dawn pick opens. When building a win, a loss, a death or a milestone, ask how it lands on screen as well as in the rules.

## Core structure

- **Run:** 30–90 minutes, procedurally generated map, randomised items, relics and events. **14 rounds** (decided 2026-10-13 with the medieval age; was 10).
- **Rounds:** each round has a decision phase and then an auto-resolved phase.
  - *Decision phase* (where the player's attention goes): placing and upgrading buildings and town layout, managing production chains and resources (Against the Storm), drafting relics, cards and event choices.
  - *Resolve phase:* combat and the simulation play out automatically (How Many Dudes / Ratropolis). The player mostly watches. No direct unit control.
- **Round shape (decided 2026-10-10):** a round is **3 fast day/night cycles**. At the start of the round the player builds. At each dawn they pick one newcomer from a few cards (How Many Dudes), and the buildings they own shape that pool. During each day heroes and townsfolk act on their own. The telegraphed wave comes on the third night; earlier nights bring only wanderers and small probes.
- **Building cards (decided 2026-10-14):** the build phase is not a full catalogue. Basic homes and food are always on offer (as in Against the Storm), and ageing up is always its own card when it can be raised. The focus is watching the town and steering with picks, not menus. **Blueprints (Yusuf, 2026-10-22, replacing the one-use dealt hand):** each build round the player drafts 1 of a few buildings, leaning toward what the town needs and the founding traditions, and a drafted building stays on offer for the rest of the run (Against the Storm's blueprints), so the town's shape is the player's build. **Crafts and chains (Yusuf, 2026-10-26, replacing rows):** a stall and its workshop are one draft card, a *craft*; the market comes as a pick of a first craft from two dealt, and each craft opens buildings no other offer brings (the chart in docs/CHAINS.md is generated from the data, never hand-kept). One offer per draft builds on what the town has (an upgrade, a building a craft opened, another craft), the others are new, so the player doesn't fish with rerolls. Everyday buildings can be built again, each copy dearer; one-of-a-kind ones say "One only". Upgrades light the buildings they change and sparkle when taken, and some bring the land back (coppicing, deep quarry, game warden). **Rarity (Yusuf, 2026-10-22):** four rarities (white base, blue magic, yellow powerful, orange mythic, data/rarity.json) shared by blueprints, items, relics and dawn cards; rarer comes up less often. **Upgrades, rule-breakers and outposts (Yusuf, 2026-10-23):** drafts may also offer an upgrade for a building you have (since 2026-10-26 as the draft's deepening offer), rare rule-breaker buildings change a rule at a cost, and outposts stand out in the wilds by a far node where raiders can wreck them. **Per-run pool (Yusuf, 2026-10-23):** each run deals its own pool of blueprints (staples, the traditions' buildings and age-up needs always in), so runs differ in which buildings they can have at all. There is no second lasting draft at dawn (Yusuf asked; dawn is busy and few weighty picks are preferred): instead each blueprint offer says what it adds to the dawn picks ("Dawn: knights can arrive"), so one choice shapes both buildings and people.
- **Map (decided 2026-10-10):** the settlement sits in the centre, with resources and threats on all sides. Monster dens far out in the wilds spawn wandering monsters. The wilds are strictly hero territory, where heroes fight for glory, loot and XP.
- **Ages (decided 2026-10-10):** runs start as a handful of tribespeople around a campfire and tech up through **neolithic, bronze and medieval**. Neolithic resources are burnable fuel (brush, dung), wood and stone, with furs and flint or obsidian for gear. **Bronze age (decided 2026-10-11):** aging up is the player's choice, a costly milestone building (power now vs. later), never automatic. Tin comes from cassiterite veins near the dens: heroes fetch it, and townsfolk can only mine it safely once heroes have cleared the nearby threats; craftspeople smelt it. A caravan may sell tin and a little cassiterite (heavy, so little of it) at a steep markup over local supply. **Medieval age (decided 2026-10-13, from the medieval plan):** a bronze town raises the **Stone keep** (player's choice, costly) to reach it. Bog iron sits inside the town's reach but smelting needs charcoal, which eats wood. Towns grow fast through family dawn cards, townhouses and fields. A guildhall draws knights, and champions can be knighted. Late threats (men-at-arms in mail, battering rams, hollow knights, the Black Margrave) arrive by round, never because the town aged up. **Ages come sooner (Yusuf, 2026-10-14):** runs stay 14 rounds, and ageing up should be reachable mid-run as a power spike you use, not something that arrives as the run ends.
- **Town layout (decided 2026-10-11):** Frostpunk-style rings of plots round the hearth, adapted rather than copied. Buildings **place themselves** (Yusuf chose auto-placement over picking cells): each type prefers a ring and neighbours, plots touch so a row of buildings is a wall, and placement never cuts the hearth off. **Clusters (Yusuf, 2026-10-19):** buildings stand in clusters of about three with a lane plot left free between clusters, in every ring; lanes line up from ring to ring (Yusuf chose lined up over staggered), and gates prefer them. Watch posts guard gates in turn: each new post goes just outside the gate with fewest posts, then over the next likely gate spot; stacking several on one gate is for later relics or setups. **Opening rings (Yusuf, 2026-10-23):** a run starts with 2 rings of plots open; each age-up opens the next for free, and once the open rings are nearly full the blueprint draft offers to clear the next ring for a flat cost of wood and stone, in place of that draft's building (power now vs room later; a bigger town has a longer edge to hold). Everyone uses real pathfinding round buildings and obstacles. **Desire paths (decided 2026-10-12, replacing staked roads and palisades, which Yusuf found too fiddly):** the player doesn't lay roads. Feet wear trails, then worn paths, where people walk most, and a stone-laying building's craftspeople pave the busiest paths for stone, with better road kinds in later ages. Roads speed raiders too. Buildings are built over trails freely and keep off paving unless nothing else is free, in which case the stones are lifted and refunded. Towers help but carry risk: sentient raiders they shoot turn on them, and a broken tower is silent until mended at dawn (Yusuf, 2026-10-14). The player's static defence is the **gate**, a building that places itself beside buildings of a ring (best in a gap between two) where the busiest path runs: their people walk through, raiders batter it or go round. **Raider routes and quick defences (Yusuf, 2026-10-27):** the player sees a faint red line draw itself along the way tonight's attack will walk, repainted in the crossing plate's look where it crosses the turning ring; townsfolk can't see it, and only once someone spots a raider do nearby tribespeople and craftspeople throw up brush barricades (weak gates, 1 wood each) in the gaps on that side, gone at dawn.
- **Shifting earth (decided 2026-10-20, Yusuf's idea; simplified 2026-10-25):** **the earth is this round's weather (Yusuf, 2026-10-25: the earth dial was overwrought and too much info).** Just past the still town lies one ring of four plates of different kinds (Fen, Greenwood, Stonefield, Barrow), and once between rounds it clicks round a whole plate (the town and the dens never move). Each night's attack crosses one plate on its way in, and the attack card names it in one line ("across the Fen: raiders wade slowly under your towers"); each kind gives and takes (raiders wade the Fen under the towers but so do gatherers, the Greenwood hides raiders but regrows, the Stonefield is open but fast, the fallen may rise on the Barrow but heroes dig grave goods there). The player never works the earth directly: relics and buildings key on plate tags through one rule (plate_knobs: Bog-drowner's Stake, Thorn-witch's Veil, Cairn-warden's Cord, Barrow-king's Lamp, the Causeway and the Lookout cairn), Stillstone holds the ring, the Lodestone Wheel turns it two plates, and the town reads the crossing by itself (watch posts lean toward the way in, bold heroes go out to meet the attack on its plate, gatherers keep off its way that day). The turn plays as a short camera pull-out (Space skips it). **Gone (2026-10-25):** the middle and far rings, seams with pressure, cracks and wakes (trenches, ridges, rifts), earth finds and earthborn gear, the Geomancer's lodge and its spells, the Feed the earth card, and outposts riding the plates. History: the turning rings (plan https://claude.ai/code/artifact/2ef9abcf-0b5c-40a5-b1a3-9e2b34695d09), making it felt (https://claude.ai/artifact/D3LCxoxQfho3uD9YnFYTbD), the earth dial (https://claude.ai/artifact/PXix3fVvKD1zoH5MdQkrci). A later slice may bring a way to steer the earth back only if it stays one glanceable line.
- **Rarity (Yusuf, 2026-10-22):** four rarities across the game, white (base), blue (magic), yellow (powerful) and orange (mythic), on items, relics, dawn cards, blueprints and caravan deals. Rarer things are offered less often, less so as the run goes on, and orange brings named mythic content (unique items, costless mythic relics, legendary heroes). **Rolled cards (Yusuf, 2026-10-23):** a dealt card also rolls its own rarity each time (about 5% blue rising to 9%, 1% to 3% yellow, 0.2% to 1% orange) and a rolled card carries modifiers: blue one, yellow two or three, orange a transmutation, a trade-off that perverts a mechanic. Higher rolls are simply better; their rarity keeps them special. The frame shows the roll and a small gem the kind's own rarity. Relics don't roll (a later brainstorm may break that rule). Slices: townsfolk dawn cards first, then other dawn cards and trades (spoils are relics, so they don't roll), then blueprints and upgrades, whose modifiers change the building for the rest of the run. **Card redesign (Yusuf, 2026-10-24, plan https://claude.ai/artifact/Y98vEUJFuTj3ZYLSBYkxQg):** every picking screen draws one card look with fixed slots: the frame is the roll, a coloured band with an icon is the kind of card, the price sits top right, icons and numbers show what you get, modifiers are short plaques (an orange one's price on its own red line), long text is on hover; the four rarity colours appear only on card frames, pips and plaques. Plan: https://claude.ai/artifact/CrxFF7wmcz36xfii2MUZNB
- **Founding traditions (decided 2026-10-20, from the market scan):** before round 1 the player picks one of six traditions (Yusuf, 2026-10-25, was two), or presses Random for a seeded one plus +5 food, stone and fuel; each tilts the building hand and the dawn's tribespeople toward its kind and brings one lasting gift, so runs start differently. **Each brings its first blueprint (Yusuf, 2026-10-26)** in place of one of round 1's two drafts (Stoneworkers a watch post). New traditions are a natural meta unlock later.
- **Run paths (Yusuf, 2026-10-27, plan https://claude.ai/artifact/6v3b5DfbBM3B4xwYJvj3Cu):** four ways to specialise a run (Heroes, Warband, Guilds, Trade), each three tiers deep, so runs can play very differently. The founding tradition gives tier I of its path, and each age-up ends on a charter pick that raises any path a tier (a pure run reaches a path's capstone; a mixed run holds two paths' early tiers). Tier II takes something from another kind of person. A path leans the draft and dawn toward its content. Slices: 1 the charter, four tier Is and trades guilds; 2 the tier IIs and their costs; 3 the capstones. **Trades guilds** come in every run: a tribesperson band at its top tier can be chartered at dawn, in place of a newcomer, and learns a practice that keeps its nodes from running dry (sustainable logging, berry tending, gentle cutting, controlled burns, careful seams); its members then keep to their trade.
- **First run (Yusuf, 2026-10-26, plan https://claude.ai/artifact/W8GAAEkSx4ntVAVyDzDNqS):** a player's first run opens on a short camera tour (a throwaway prologue of the same seed: a hero in the wilds, the hearth, the town, a den, the map) instead of the round screen, then starts gently (the first dawn offers a couple of tribespeople only, no reroll on the first draft). One-time tips are hi-viz highlights round the part of the screen they're about, one at a time, closing when the player does the thing. The top bar grows with the town (shells, relics and the rest appear when they first matter).
- **Pressure:** escalating enemy waves **and** environmental hardship and scarcity. Social and moral pressure (factions, morale, Frostpunk-style choices) is a *later* layer, added only once the town's internal mechanics are deep enough to support it. **Mercy is a loan (Yusuf, 2026-10-26, plan https://claude.ai/artifact/Wph93ouiggRtrmz8he3YUR):** catch-up still holds raiders back from an attack that far outmatches the town (never more than a quarter of them), but they make a war camp that marches with the next round's attack unless heroes break it first, and breaking it pays no plunder. Instead, holding an attack the warband didn't need to ease earns **glory**: one more relic to choose from. So falling behind costs later, and keeping pace pays.
- **Victory conditions:** a mix, such as surviving waves, reaching economic or production goals, or collecting or completing certain relics and items. Vary them per run or map.

## NPCs

- **Heroes:** individuals with traits, personalities, morals and itemisation (equipment, loot). They act on their own incentives: they go out to fight, explore and loot based on their goals, and they react to bounties, buildings and town state. They should feel like characters whose stories emerge.
- **Townspeople / colonists:** mostly generic and handled **in aggregate**. They're modified, transformed and upgraded as groups (Against the Storm's races are a reference for group needs), not micromanaged one by one. Newcomers arrive through the dawn pick (for example tribesperson, warrior or craftsperson), and each one adds upkeep. A sufficiently upgraded townsperson can be promoted to an individual with traits and gear.
- **Hero recruitment:** heroes need lodging (a guild, tavern or similar). Once you have it, heroes appear as dawn cards with an extra gold recruitment cost. Heroes are scarce and capricious, and they spend energy on their own agendas, then rest at lodging that suits them.
- **Incentives come from buildings:** bounties and prizes are posted by buildings that have prerequisites, such as a hunting lodge posting prizes on beasts. There is no free bounty button.
- **Economy:** the marketplace sells goods and services directly to heroes, and each stall is fed by an upstream supply chain of buildings.
- Heroes do the **bulk of the fighting**. Static defences around the town and castle (walls, towers) help but shouldn't carry the game.

## Items and relics

- Sources: reward picks after waves or milestones (Slay the Spire), map events and exploration, and town production or markets where heroes buy gear (Majesty, Moonlighter).
- Heroes should ideally make their own loot and gear choices (automated by their personality and needs). The player shapes this indirectly.
- Relics define run-level builds and should create synergies and the power-now-or-later trade-off.

## Meta-progression (between runs)

- **Unlocks that add variety:** new buildings, hero types, relics and events (Against the Storm, Hades). Prefer variety over raw permanent stat boosts.
- **Hub town** that you build up between runs (Cult of the Lamb, Moonlighter).
- **Story across runs** (Hades-style) is a *later* goal. Don't build it now, but don't design it out.

## Art and tone

- **For now: simple placeholder art.** Keep visuals swappable (no logic coupled to specific art assets).
- Final style is undecided. The candidates are **pixel art** or **low-poly 3D**, chosen for ease of development. Claude should help evaluate the options when the prototype is playable.
- **Setting and tone (decided 2026-10-10):** historical fiction with dark fantasy mixed in, kept a little mystical. A people rises from stone to steel across the three ages.

## Technical direction

- **Engine:** Godot 4.x (GDScript unless there's a strong reason for C#).
- **Platform:** PC, targeting Steam.
- **Goal:** mostly a hobby and learning project, but build it to **SDLC practices compatible with a future Steam release**:
  - Git with meaningful commits, feature branches and PRs.
  - Automated tests for core simulation logic (e.g. GUT for Godot) and CI (GitHub Actions) that runs them, plus headless export builds.
  - Data-driven content (buildings, heroes, relics, items and enemies defined in resource/data files, not hardcoded) so balancing and modding stay easy.
  - Keep simulation logic separate from presentation, so the auto-resolve phase can run headless for tests and balance simulations.
  - **Tags (proposed by Yusuf 2026-10-10):** entities carry descriptive tags in their data (e.g. a wolf is `animal`, `sentient`, `beast`), and rules key on a few of them (sentient creatures turn on whoever hits them). Descriptive tags are fine (decided 2026-10-11), and upcoming relics, hero abilities, buildings and mechanics will key on tags. A tag only does something when a rule reads it, and every tag a rule reads is explained to players in `data/tags.json`, which hover tooltips show (see docs/DEVELOPMENT.md).
  - Deterministic, seedable RNG, so runs are reproducible for debugging and balancing.
  - Versioned save formats, settings, and input remapping. Plan for the Steamworks integration points (achievements, cloud saves) without building them early.
- **Yusuf's background:** low familiarity with code. They understand SDLC concepts from years around software projects, but have written very little code and no end-to-end programs. Claude owns build, test, troubleshoot and deploy end to end. Explain technical choices in plain language, never ask Yusuf to debug or edit code, and give one-click or one-command ways to run things.

## Working in the code (read first)

**[docs/DEVELOPMENT.md](docs/DEVELOPMENT.md) is the code map and the habits every change follows.** The short version:
- `sim/` is the game and runs headless; `game/` only shows it and never changes rules. Content and numbers live in `data/*.json`, read through `Content`, each file with a `_note` explaining its keys.
- Tags: any entity (enemy, hero class, trait, townsfolk role, building, item base, relic, resource node) can carry descriptive tags, and relics, abilities, buildings and mechanics key on tags rather than ids. Rules read them only via `Content.has_tag` / `unit_has_tag`; a tag a rule reads gets a player-facing line in `data/tags.json` (`tests/test_tags.gd` enforces it).
- All randomness goes through `SeededRng`. Run records only grow: new fields, bump `RECORD_FORMAT`, update `tools/export_csv.py`.
- Every rule gets a test; balance-affecting changes report bot win rates; new mechanics are taught to `AutoPlayer`.
- Setup and tests: `tools/setup_godot.sh` once (cloud sessions do it at start), then `tools/test.sh [filter]` (also fails on script errors); bot win rates with `tools/balance.sh`. Claude's pending design defaults and their numbers are in docs/PENDING_DEFAULTS.md. New behaviour goes in the matching `World` part (`FolkAI`, `HeroAI`, `Market`, `EnemyAI`) and new UI in its own `game/` file.

## Fast iteration (dev-cycle priority)

Yusuf must be able to try changes and give feedback quickly, without heavy builds. This keeps token cost down and keeps development interactive.
- **Small slices:** ship the smallest playable change that answers a design question, then get feedback before building more.
- **Playtest in the browser:** Yusuf plays the web build at the link in README.md (every merge to `main` goes live there), with no install or build steps on their side. Fall back to a downloadable desktop build only when web can't do it.
- **Tune without rebuilding:** balance numbers live in data files, and in-game debug or tuning panels let Yusuf tweak values and re-run a round live.
- **Prototype before engine work** when it's cheaper: paper or spreadsheet mockups, or text summaries of headless simulation runs (e.g. "100 seeded runs: average waves survived, top relics"), to settle a design question before building it in-game.
- **Clear feedback hooks:** each playtest comes with 2-3 specific questions, plus a seed so Yusuf can say "seed 4821, round 6 felt unfair" and Claude can reproduce it.
- **Run history:** every run is saved as a versioned record (`RunState.to_record()`): the choices made, each wave, hero snapshots, deaths, relics and the result. Yusuf copies runs from the in-game Run history screen into chat, and simulated runs use the same format, so `tools/run_report.gd` can compare them. When mechanics change, extend the record and bump `RECORD_FORMAT` rather than dropping fields.
- Keep each iteration's report short: what changed, how to try it, what to look for.

## How to work with Yusuf

- **Default mode:** Claude proposes designs and builds. Yusuf approves big decisions and playtests.
- **Be token-efficient.** Keep replies short, lead with the decision or result, and skip narrating the investigation.
- **Decision policy (evolving):** to start, check in with Yusuf on design decisions. Then keep evaluating which *kinds* of decisions can be offloaded, and propose moving them into the "Claude decides" list below. Once Yusuf agrees, record them there and stop asking.
- When asking, give short multiple-choice options with a recommendation marked, grounded in the reference games where helpful.
- Pick sensible defaults for low-stakes implementation details and say which were chosen.
- **Don't punish optimising (Yusuf, 2026-10-12):** as a general theme, scaling and pressure systems may ease off for a struggling town but never get harder because the player is doing well.
