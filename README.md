# Your Earth Keeps Turning (working title)

Hobbyist city-builder roguelite, co-built with Claude. Design intent lives in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md).

## Play it

Every change merged to `main` is published as a browser build:
**https://carltonbonks.github.io/YourEarthKeepsTurning-CityBuilder/**

No install needed. Desktop Chrome, Edge or Firefox recommended.
Add `?seed=1234` to the address to replay a specific run. Add `&dev=1` for developer tools such as skipping ahead.

The screen is kept short: a thin bar of key numbers on top, the map, and your heroes and the latest story underneath. Point at anything (a number on the bar, a hero, a card, something on the map) for the detail. Space pauses, Esc opens the menu (seed, new run, Run history), V tilts the map. The **Impact** button under Log shows who did what, this round or over the whole run: damage each hero, kind of townsfolk, the hearth and the towers dealt and took, kills, townsfolk lost, and the work each kind did (goods brought home, things made, buildings raised, paving, hearth mended). The round-survived screen leads with its headline.

### How it plays

The game opens on a title screen: New run, Play a seed, How to play, Run history, Settings (fullscreen, interface size, the speed runs start at, the night camera, tips, and moving the keys) and Quit on desktop. A playtest link with `?seed=` skips it. Every run opens on one screen that explains a round, and the first time each thing happens (the first build, dawn, night, relic, hero card, turn of the earth and age card) a short tip points it out; each shows once, and Settings can show them again.

A handful of people around a fire, in the middle of a map. Resources ring the camp, monster dens sit far out in the wilds. Survive 14 rounds. Before round 1, pick one **founding tradition** of six (hunters, woodfolk, stoneworkers, firekeepers, traders, smiths), or press Random to let fate choose one and start with +5 food, stone and fuel: it makes its buildings come up more often in the building hand, keeps its kind of tribesperson coming at dawn (they band one sooner), and brings one lasting gift and some starting goods. Each tradition also starts you on one of four **paths** (Heroes, Warband, Guilds or Trade): a hero card every dawn, a warrior card every dawn with warriors eating half, one more dawn card with bands forming sooner, or the caravan from round 1 with rarer deals and more shells. Each time you age up you choose a **charter** that adds another path or raises one you hold to **tier II**, which brings a new rule and takes something from another kind of person: Heroes II keeps a fallen hero's best item as an heirloom for the next hero and adds half again drill XP (one fewer tribesperson card each dawn); Warband II has tribespeople take up spears at half strength on the attack's night and barricades the attack's way at dusk (warriors stop keeping watch over gatherers); Guilds II charters bands of 4 and makes every workshop 30% faster (hero cards come half as often); Trade II has the caravan offer hired blades every round, three warriors for that round only who need no beds (tribesperson and warrior cards cost 3 shells). Capstones (tier III) come later. When six tribespeople of one leaning form a full band, a dawn card offers to charter them as a **trades guild**: woodcutters log sustainably, foragers tend the berries, brushcutters burn in season (the nodes they work regrow each dawn), and quarriers and prospectors cut gently (their rocks and veins drain slower), but guild members keep to their trade from then on. Each round:
1. **Build.** A hand of building cards lies along the bottom of the map: homes and food are always on offer, and every other building is a **blueprint** you draft. Each round you draft 1 of 3 offers (2 in the first round, and an extra one when you reach a new age), and a drafted building stays yours for the whole run. Each run deals its own pool of blueprints: about half of the buildings, plus homes, lodgings, watch posts, gates, your traditions' buildings and what ageing up needs, so another run offers a different set (hover the draft's title to see this run's). A card the town needs says why at the top (for example "Recommended: food runs out in 2 days"), and an offer says what it adds to the dawn picks ("Dawn: heroes can arrive"). Order huts, watch posts, garden patches (a little food every dawn), drying racks, hero lodgings (a longhouse, a mead hall or a spirit shrine), and the market and its crafts. The market comes with your first **craft**: the draft offers two, and each is a workshop and the stall that sells its goods (arms, hides, charms or provisions). A craft opens buildings no other draft brings (the hides craft opens the hunting lodge and the kennel), and later drafts can offer more crafts. One offer in every draft builds on what you have (an upgrade, a building your craft opened or another craft) and the others are new. Card footers say how many you may build: "One only", "Up to 4", or "Build again" for everyday buildings such as workshops and foresters, each copy costing half again more. Craftspeople raise them during the day, and each building picks its own plot in the rings round the hearth, in clusters of about three with a lane left free between clusters (lanes line up from ring to ring, so people and gates have room): homes and lodgings near the fire, stalls and workshops round the market, each new watch post just outside a gate that has none yet (or over the next likely gate spot), the hunting lodge and stone-layers' yard on the edge, and a gate beside buildings (best in a gap between two) where the busiest path runs. Blueprints have a rarity, shown by the colour of their name: white (base), blue (magic), yellow (powerful) and orange (mythic); rarer ones come up less often and do stronger or stranger things, such as the trophy hall's prizes for great beasts, a kennel's hounds for your best heroes or the seer's extra dawn card. Once a building stands, the draft may offer an **upgrade** for it (sturdy huts hold 4, tall watch posts shoot farther, practised knappers work twice as fast), which changes every one you have for the rest of the run: hovering it lights those buildings on the map, and taking it makes them sparkle. Some upgrades bring the land back: coppicing doubles the forester's regrowth, a deep quarry makes rocks round the stone-layers' yard regrow, and a game warden at the hunting lodge tends the wild plants round it. Some rare blueprints are **rule-breakers** with a real cost (a bone pit raises the night's first fallen enemies to fight for you but burns fuel; an oath stone makes heroes stronger now but slower to learn), and **outposts** stand out in the wilds by the farthest grove, rocks, berries or tin, bringing goods in every dawn until a raider or monster wrecks them or the earth takes their node. Plots touch, so a row of buildings is a wall that everyone (raiders too) has to walk round. Sentient raiders a watch post shoots may turn on it; a broken post stops shooting until it is mended at dawn. A card in the map's top-right corner shows where the round's attack will come from and your town's power against it; the Begin round button sits at the right end of the hand. Hover over anything on the map or a card for what it does, its tags, and warnings such as a stall with no workshop feeding it.
2. **Three days and nights.** At each dawn, pick one newcomer from a few cards (tribespeople, warrior, craftsperson, or a hero once any lodging stands). Tribespeople come with a leaning (forager, woodcutter, quarrier, brushcutter, and prospector from the bronze age): they still fetch whatever the town is short of, but go for their own goods first. Three sharing a leaning form a band and bring 1 more of their goods a trip; six also gather half again as fast. A card that would form or strengthen a band is marked in gold. A townsperson who stands out can be offered as a Promote card instead: a veteran warrior becomes a champion hero, a busy craftsperson a named master. Now and then one card is a **venture** instead: send a few tribespeople out (cull the warthogs, float timber downriver, climb the far cliffs, and later trek for tin or raid a hill clan's herds); the card says how many may not come back, and the rest bring the goods home at once. Then everyone acts on their own: tribespeople gather (what they carry home rises over the hearth as "+6 wood", gathered every second and a half; one leaning to a good takes over a trip for it from one who doesn't) and patch the hearth. With nothing the town is short of in reach, they lend a hand in town instead: tending gardens and fields for extra food, helping at a workshop that suits their leaning, or helping raise buildings (craftspeople help at busy workshops too). Warriors patrol, and by day half of them keep watch over gatherers working out past the houses; a gatherer with a warrior beside it walks 12% faster while no foe is near (a green diamond at its feet). Heroes follow the day's whim (hunt, guard, sleep in, carouse) and hunt at the dens for loot and XP; those who don't roam drill by the hearth on quiet days for a little XP, and up to two patrolling warriors drill beside each. After about a day's drilling a warrior earns a lasting **mark** from the hero's class: swift beside a stalker (+10% speed, green diamond), hardy beside a champion or knight (+15% health, blue circle), sharp beside a hunter (+10% attack, red square); dutiful heroes teach twice as fast. Hover a townsperson to see their mark and who taught it. Everyone, heroes included, eats 1 food each dawn, and heroes eat a hearty extra helping while the store still holds two days of food, which makes them earn 30% more XP that day; if there isn't enough, a townsperson walks away, heroes sulk, and nobody of that kind comes to that dawn's pick. Each townsfolk card says how that kind spent the round: **Stretched** (more work than hands, so another earns their keep), **Busy** or **Slack** (mostly idle, so another mostly adds upkeep). A **Reeve's hut** (a blueprint every run can draw) opens the **People** panel (how each kind and leaning spent the round and what they brought in) and each townsperson's day on hover, and deals more of the people you are stretched for.
3. **Getting around.** Everyone finds their own way round buildings, boulders and old oaks, and around the thickets out in the wilds. Ground people walk a lot wears into a trail, and a busy trail into a worn path (each a little faster); idle ones fade. A stone-layers' yard lets craftspeople pave the busiest worn paths for stone (much faster, and cobbles in the bronze age). Raiders use paths and roads too. A gate across a busy path lets your people through but raiders must batter it down or go round; each gate gets a watch post before any gate gets a second. A faint red line draws itself on the map from the build until the attack sets off, along the way tonight's raiders will walk (round your buildings and through the gaps and gates they'll hit); where it crosses the turning ring it takes on that plate's look (wavy on the Fen, faint dots in the Greenwood, bold chevrons on the Stonefield, crosses on the Barrow) with a few words saying what that ground does, so you see the crossing change when the ring turns. Your people can't see that line: once someone (a townsperson, a hero, a watch post or the hearth) spots a raider coming, a "!" shows over them and nearby tribespeople and craftspeople run to throw up **barricades** of brush and timber in up to 3 gaps on that side (1 wood each). A barricade is a weak gate: raiders batter it or go round. They come down at dawn. Raiders are seen later in the Greenwood's cover and sooner on the open Stonefield. New buildings go over trails freely and keep off paving, lifting the stones (back into stock) only when nothing else is free. The bronze age opens a fourth ring of plots.
4. **Heroes run on energy and mood.** They tire as they work and fight, go to bed when tired and at nightfall, and only wake when an enemy comes close. Each personality has a favourite lodging. Hunger, cold, sleeping rough and comrades dying sour their mood; a favourite bed, shopping and prizes lift it. An unhappy hero walks away.
5. **The market.** Stalls (arms, armour, charms, provisions) sell to heroes, fed by workshops (knapper, hide rack, bone carver, smoke hut) that craftspeople staff. A hunting lodge pays heroes a prize for every beast. A **runners' hut** (after the provisions stall) sends tribespeople from the town out to heroes in the wilds who run out of food or poultices: the hero pays the stall price and a 3-shell fee and keeps hunting instead of walking home. Runners stop at dusk and can be caught by monsters. Every third round a **pedlar** passes: you invite one of three (a tinker who camps close by, an armourer or charm-seller a fair walk out, a blade-trader far out between the dens), or none. The card says which heroes would walk that far. The pedlar camps out there for the round's days and leaves before the attack; heroes walk out on their own to sell their loot (the pedlar takes no cut, the market keeps a fifth) and buy wares. The pedlar hires a dutiful or greedy hero or two as guards, who sleep out at the camp and are paid in wares; raiders that come near go for the camp, and if it falls the pedlar flees with the wares.
6. **The bronze age.** Raise the Bronze moot (costly, your choice when) to unlock copper seams, the smelter, the bronzesmith, bronze tool sets for townsfolk groups and a southern caravan: each round it lays out a few **trade cards** of four rarities (white, blue, yellow, orange) in the Trading section of the building hand: bulk lots of tin, timber or stone, barters (hides for tin, surplus food for shells), services (sellswords, journeymen, a smith who reforges every hero's weapon) and rare finds (a relic dealer, a caravan family that settles). Take any you can pay for, once each. Heroes fetch cassiterite from near the dens. Bronze reavers arrive from round 6 and shrug off stone blows.
7. **The medieval age.** Once the bronzesmith stands and 12 people live in town, raise the Stone keep (it shoots, and adds health to the hearth) to open a fifth ring of plots, bog iron in the marshes, the charcoal burner, bloomery and blacksmith (iron, then steel), townhouses, fields worked by tribespeople, family cards at dawn (several newcomers at once), the guildhall (knights answer the call, and a veteran champion can be knighted) and the chapel. From round 10 come men-at-arms in mail, battering rams that ignore people and go for gates and the hearth, and hollow knights from the barrows; steel cuts through their mail. The Black Margrave leads the last attack.
8. **Night 3 brings the warned attack.** Survive it, then **pick a relic** (1 of 3), or instead take the **spoils** of the kind of enemy the attack was made of (a fourth card): goods for every raider, beast, barrow-dead or ram that came, a raider who joins as a warrior, or gear made from them. Most relics change a rule rather than a number (warriors that grow stronger in a horde, fallen raiders who rise to fight for you, the earth turning twice), and every fourth round offers **pacts**: strong relics with a real cost. Attacks grow every round on a fixed schedule; if your town has fallen far behind, the warband holds some raiders back (the story tells you), but a strong town never faces a bigger attack for being strong. Held-back raiders don't vanish: they make a war camp out in the wilds by one of your resources, set on anyone who comes close, and stop townsfolk gathering near it until heroes clear it, which brings back shells of plunder and an item for the hero who ends it.

## Layout

| Folder | What lives there |
| --- | --- |
| `sim/` | Game rules and simulation. No graphics, so it can run headless for tests and balance runs. |
| `game/` | Scenes, UI and visuals that show the simulation. |
| `data/` | Balance numbers and content (heroes, enemies, relics) as JSON files. |
| `tools/` | Headless helpers, e.g. the balance report and Godot setup. |
| `docs/` | [DEVELOPMENT.md](docs/DEVELOPMENT.md): code map and development habits. [PENDING_DEFAULTS.md](docs/PENDING_DEFAULTS.md): Claude's design defaults still waiting for a playtest. [PERFORMANCE.md](docs/PERFORMANCE.md): how the sim copes with big towns. |
| `tests/` | Automated tests for `sim/`. |

## For developers

Engine: Godot 4.7.1 (GDScript). Read [docs/DEVELOPMENT.md](docs/DEVELOPMENT.md) first. `tools/setup_godot.sh` downloads Godot to `~/godot/godot` (Linux); the CI workflow in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` shows the exact commands.

```sh
tools/setup_godot.sh                                     # get Godot (Linux), once
tools/test.sh                                            # import and run every test (tools/test.sh market: only matching tests)
tools/balance.sh 60 balanced workers                     # bot win rates, styles in parallel
godot --headless --path . --export-release "Web" build/web/index.html
godot --headless --path . -s res://tools/balance_run.gd -- runs=50 style=balanced   # balance report
```

Run history: the game saves every run (Run history button). Copy one into chat, or compare many:

```sh
godot --headless --path . -s res://tools/balance_run.gd -- runs=50 style=balanced out=sim.jsonl
godot --headless --path . -s res://tools/run_report.gd -- in=sim.jsonl in=my_runs.json
```

Balance report styles (a scripted player): `balanced` (huts, a longhouse, watch posts, picks by need), `workers` (never takes heroes), `heroes` (rushes the longhouse, takes every hero it can afford), `market` (builds every workshop, stall and the hunting lodge early), `bronze` (like market, then ages up as soon as it can), `medieval` (like bronze, then raises the Stone keep and uses the medieval buildings). `tools/market_probe.gd -- seed=4821 style=market` prints one run's economy round by round. `tools/snapshot.gd` saves a picture of the screen after a bot has played some rounds (needs a display; see the file).
